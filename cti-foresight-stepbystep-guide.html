Facilitation Guide / Future Threat Surface

CTI Foresight Workshop: Step by Step

A granular run guide for the five-method foresight pipeline. Each step has a procedure, a diagram, a worked example on the financial-sector threat surface, and the failure modes to watch.

HALF DAY / ~4 HOURS 6 ANALYSTS OUTPUT → JUNE 16 DECK
  1. Scanning
  2. Drivers & uncertainty
  3. Scenarios via 2x2
  4. Tail risk
  5. Indicators & calibration
Step 01

Scanning

Turn six analysts' pre-work signals into one shared, de-duplicated set of named drivers, with cross-category links the individual scans could not see.

In: per-analyst STEEP-M scans (3-5 sourced signals each) Out: 8-12 named drivers Time: 35 min

Run it

  1. 2 minRestate the question and the trend-versus-shift distinction. State the two rules out loud: every signal needs a source, and every signal needs a one-line "so what."
  2. 5 minSilent post. One signal per sticky note. Each analyst posts theirs under their STEEP-M category heading on the wall. One signal per sticky forces atomic, movable units.
  3. 3 minRound-robin read-out. Each analyst gives a 30-second tour of their two strongest signals. No debate yet. This gets every voice in before the loudest one sets the frame.
  4. 8 minAffinity cluster. As a group, physically move stickies that point to the same underlying force together. Push for cross-category clusters. Prompt repeatedly: "what force is underneath these?"
  5. 5 minName each cluster as a driver, phrased as a force, not an event. Write the name on a card above the cluster. Aim for 8 to 12 drivers.
  6. 2 minDebrief. Ask what clustering revealed that no single category saw. Park orphan signals that did not cluster; they are wild-card candidates for Step 4.

Worked example

"Agentic AI lowers the cost of tailored phishing" (Technology) and "third-party SaaS concentration rising" (Attack surface) cluster into a single driver: the cost of credible intrusion is falling while our dependency surface widens.

Watch-outs

Analysts stay in their lane and miss cross-category links. The "so what" line is where weak signals die, so enforce it. Resist the pull to write drivers as events; a driver is a force that shapes many events.

Step 02

Drivers & uncertainty

Separate the near-certain baseline from the genuinely uncertain forces, and pick the two high-impact, high-uncertainty drivers that will become your scenario axes.

In: 8-12 named drivers Out: baseline trend list + 2 scenario axes Time: 25-30 min

Run it

  1. 3 minTeach the two dimensions. Impact is how much the threat surface changes if this force moves. Uncertainty is how confidently you can predict its direction over the horizon. They are independent.
  2. 5 minDraw the large grid on a whiteboard. Horizontal axis is uncertainty, vertical axis is impact.
  3. 7 minPlace each driver. Hand out dots and let the team place drivers, or place by quick group consensus. Use dot spread to expose genuine disagreement rather than papering over it.
  4. 5 minRead the grid. Top-left, high impact and low uncertainty, is your baseline. Top-right, high impact and high uncertainty, holds your axis candidates. The bottom half waits.
  5. 5 minSelect two axes from the top-right that are highest impact, genuinely uncertain, and independent of each other.
  6. 2 minSanity check. State each axis as a spectrum with two plausible poles. If you can confidently predict which pole wins, it is baseline, not an axis.

Worked example

"Regulatory disclosure pressure increases" lands high-impact, low-uncertainty, so it is baseline. "Does AI shift net advantage to attackers or defenders" and "is critical dependency concentrated or distributed" both land high-impact, high-uncertainty, and are independent of each other, so they become the two axes.

Watch-outs

Important-but-predictable drivers love to masquerade as uncertainties; demote them to baseline. Correlated axes are the most common failure here and they wreck Step 3.

Step 03 / Centerpiece

Scenarios via 2x2

Build four plausible, distinct futures from the two axes, each describing a different threat surface, then stress-test them so they survive a leadership room.

In: 2 independent axes Out: 4 named scenarios + red-team critique Time: 75 min

Run it

  1. 5 minDraw the 2x2 and label all four poles. Make clear each quadrant is a world where both drivers have resolved a particular way.
  2. 10 minWe-do. Build one quadrant together as a live demo: name it, sketch what is true in this world, what the threat surface looks like, what attacks get cheap, and where BlackRock is exposed.
  3. 30 minYou-do. Split into pairs and assign quadrants. Each pair produces a memorable name, a 3-4 sentence narrative, the dominant threats, BlackRock's exposure, and the "leading domino," the first thing that would happen on the path to this world.
  4. 15 minGallery walk. Each pair presents its quadrant in three minutes.
  5. 10 minRed-team pass. A designated red hat attacks each scenario for internal consistency, distinctness from its neighbors, and blind spots. Capture every challenge.
  6. 5 minHarvest. List shifts that appear in more than one quadrant (robust) versus those unique to one (contingent). This list feeds Step 5.

Worked example

Axes of AI advantage and dependency concentration yield four worlds: Brittle Monoculture (attacker-favored and concentrated), Defensible Core (defender-favored and concentrated), Diffuse Skirmish (attacker-favored and distributed), and Resilient Mesh (defender-favored and distributed).

Watch-outs

Scenarios collapsing into "good versus bad" signals correlated axes. Narratives drifting into prediction misses the point; each quadrant is a conditional. And writing four lightly reskinned versions of today wastes the method.

Step 04

Tail risk

Surface the high-impact, low-probability discontinuities the four scenarios missed, and use imagined failure to expose blind-spot shifts. This is where the "be bold" material comes from.

In: 4 scenarios + parked orphan signals Out: wild-card list + pre-mortem causal chains Time: 30 min

Run it — wild cards

  1. 3 minTeach. Wild cards are low-probability, high-consequence events. Low probability is the entry ticket, not a disqualifier. Pull the parked orphan signals from Step 1 back into view.
  2. 7 minSilent brainstorm, then round-robin. Each person names one discontinuity none of the four scenarios captured. Ban the word "unlikely" during this round.
  3. 5 minPlot on a probability-by-impact grid and keep only the high-impact corner.

Run it — pre-mortem

  1. 2 minSet the frame. "It is 2028. BlackRock has taken a defining InfoSec hit that made the news. It already happened."
  2. 8 minSilent write. Each person writes the headline and the proximate cause. Reasoning backward from a fixed failure beats forward speculation for surfacing blind spots.
  3. 5 minShare and extract. For each compelling pre-mortem, trace the chain back to an early observable. That observable is a candidate indicator, and the failure maps to a shift.

Worked example

A pre-mortem produces: a trusted analytics vendor embedded in the core platform supply chain is compromised and used as a launch point. The backward chain (unreviewed third-party code in a privileged context, anomalous update cadence) exposes a shift: trust concentrates in a few deeply embedded vendors.

Watch-outs

The group will try to kill wild cards as too unlikely; that is why you ban the word. Pre-mortems that blame generic "human error" are useless; push for a specific shift. And do not let it become doom theater. Every pre-mortem must yield an indicator.

Step 05

Indicators & calibration

Convert the harvested shifts into monitorable claims with explicit confidence, so what leaves the room is defensible and trackable rather than speculative.

In: harvested shifts (robust, contingent, tail) Out: 5-7 shifts, each with 2-3 indicators, a probability, a horizon Time: 30 min + ongoing

Run it

  1. 8 minConverge. Vote the scenario harvest and tail-risk list down to 5-7 candidate shifts. State each as a one-line claim about a change in the threat surface.
  2. 10 minIndicators. For each shift ask, "what would we observe in the next 6 to 18 months if this is happening?" Write 2-3 concrete, collectible indicators. Test each: "more AI attacks" fails, "first confirmed financial-sector breach attributed to agentic tooling" passes. Assign an owner per shift.
  3. 8 minCalibrate. Restate each shift with an explicit probability and time horizon. Start from an outside-view base rate, then adjust for specifics. Record every forecast.
  4. 4 minReveal the sealed pre-work forecasts and compare. Where did the room move, and where did it anchor? Carry the live disagreements into the deck as honest analytic-confidence language.

Worked example

Shift: AI lowers the cost of tailored intrusion. Indicators: a measurable rise in high-quality spear-phishing against mid-tier financial firms; underground listings for AI-assisted intrusion tooling; the first breach attributed to agentic tooling. Claim: 60% chance that within three years an AI-assisted intrusion is the proximate cause of a material breach at a top-10 asset manager.

Watch-outs

Vague indicators are the most common failure; they must be specific and collectible. Confidence is not calibration, and you only learn calibration by tracking outcomes over time. The forecast record is the first thing dropped under time pressure and the thing with the most evidence behind it, so protect it.
